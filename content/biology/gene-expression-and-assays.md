## Article

Two words that come up constantly in biology seminars: *expression* (what a cell is making) and *assay* (how anyone measures it).

### From gene to protein

A gene is a stretch of DNA that encodes a protein. **Protein expression** (or gene expression) is the pipeline that turns that instruction into a working protein:

1. **Transcription.** The gene's DNA sequence is copied into **messenger RNA** (mRNA).
2. **Translation.** **Ribosomes** read the mRNA and assemble amino acids into a protein chain according to the genetic code, three nucleotides (one codon) per amino acid. The chain then folds into the protein's 3D shape.

Information flows DNA → RNA → protein. A gene is **expressed** when the cell is actively running this pipeline for it, and is dormant otherwise.

### Same genome, different cells

Every cell in the body carries the same DNA. A liver cell and a neuron look and behave completely differently because they **express different subsets of genes**. Which genes are switched on, and how strongly, is what distinguishes cell types, and also what changes when a cell responds to a drug or becomes diseased.

### Expression levels as a readout

When a paper reports **protein expression levels**, it has measured how much of a given protein a cell or tissue is producing. This is usually a readout: is the gene turned up or down by some condition, such as a drug treatment, a disease state or a mutation? "Overexpressed in tumors" means more of the protein is present in tumor cells than in comparable normal cells.

In structural-biology talks, "we expressed and purified the protein" has a more practical meaning: the gene was put into a host organism (often bacteria) so that it makes large amounts of the protein, which is then purified for experiments.

### Assays

An **assay** is a standardized test that measures or detects something: how much of X is present, or whether X is active. It turns something you cannot see directly (an enzyme working, a protein's abundance, a drug killing cells) into a number, usually via a color change, fluorescence or radioactivity that an instrument can quantify. Common types:

- **Binding assay**: does molecule A stick to molecule B, and how tightly?
- **Activity assay**: is an enzyme working, and how fast? Typically one measures how quickly it converts [substrate into product](#/note/enzyme-active-sites).
- **Expression assay**: how much of a given protein is a cell making?
- **Cell viability assay**: are the cells alive and healthy, e.g. after drug treatment?

An **inhibition assay** is an activity assay run with and without a candidate compound. "We ran an inhibition assay on spermidine synthase" means: they tested how well a compound blocks that enzyme by measuring how much product forms with versus without the compound. This is the first test of a potential drug against a target such as the [polyamine pathway](#/note/polyamines-and-cancer).

## Cards

Q: What are the two steps of protein expression?
A: **Transcription**: the gene's DNA is copied into messenger RNA (mRNA). **Translation**: ribosomes read the mRNA and assemble amino acids into a protein chain according to the genetic code.
See: From gene to protein

Q: Every cell in the body carries the same DNA. Why do a liver cell and a neuron behave so differently?
A: They express different subsets of genes. A gene is "expressed" when the cell is actively using it to make protein, as opposed to keeping it dormant.
See: Same genome, different cells

Q: A paper reports "protein expression levels" before and after a drug treatment. What was measured, and what is it usually a readout of?
A: How much of a given protein the cell or tissue was producing. It is a readout of whether the corresponding gene is turned up or down by the condition (drug, disease state, mutation).
See: Expression levels as a readout

Q: What does an assay do for a quantity you cannot observe directly?
A: It converts it (an enzyme working, a protein's abundance, cell survival) into a measurable readout such as a color change, fluorescence or radioactivity that an instrument can quantify.
See: Assays

Q: Which kind of assay asks whether an enzyme is working and how fast, and what does it measure?
A: An **activity assay**. It measures the rate at which the enzyme converts substrate into product.
See: Assays

Q: Someone says "we ran an inhibition assay on spermidine synthase". What did they do?
A: They tested how well a compound blocks the enzyme, by measuring how much product forms with versus without the compound present.
See: Assays

Q: Which assay would you run to see whether a drug kills cancer cells in a dish?
A: A **cell viability assay**: it measures whether the cells are alive and healthy after treatment.

## Quiz

Q: A lab wants to know how tightly a candidate drug sticks to its target protein, whether or not it blocks the protein's activity. Which assay fits?
- [x] A binding assay
- [ ] An activity assay
- [ ] An expression assay
- [ ] A cell viability assay
E: A binding assay asks whether A sticks to B and how tightly. An activity (or inhibition) assay would measure the functional effect instead, which is a different question.
See: Assays

Q: In protein expression, **translation** is the step in which…
- [ ] the gene's DNA is copied into mRNA
- [x] ribosomes read mRNA and assemble the corresponding chain of amino acids
- [ ] the DNA is copied before cell division
- [ ] the cell decides which genes to keep dormant
E: Copying DNA into mRNA is transcription, the first step. Copying DNA before division is replication, which is not part of expression.
See: From gene to protein
